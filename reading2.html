Reading 2


AI Slop distorting reality


The reading focuses on two parallel worlds one rooted in reality and the other of AI-generated “slop.” What has ramped up more throughout this year and the past few months, AI generated images online have become more and more rampant. Images of celebrities in funny costumes and cartoon characters eating food. However it is not just funny images and dreamy looking houses, AI is being used as propaganda. Propaganda has always existed but with AI, a tool you can use to image anything, propaganda has no restrictions and constraints. Propaganda is no longer restricted to the laborers and artists who make it but now anyone can make it. The readings example of AI generated images show blonde families as the ideal future. This is problematic in many ways as AI is fed data that is also biased and tends to reproduce conservative, hierarchical norms. This demonstrates that AI is not neutral. It becomes a feedback loop, reinforcing racialized and gendered ideals as technological innovation. In this reading there was an example of an elderly relative who believed AI images of Sudan’s war. This shows how AI spreads in private messaging channels and social media platforms. There, content comes with the authority of trust. If a friend or family member sends a video, the bond you have with a person, especially if you look up to them, you dont tend to question if what they send or post is real.
Another powerful idea in the reading is desensitization. When horrifying real events of real people burning in Gaza and deportees in cages are seen after AI images of politicians as babies or fantasy cabins in the snow, everything becomes part of the same endless scroll. The algorithm and the use of “doom scrolling” reduces tragedy to just “content.” I find this deeply disturbing because it shows the problem is not ignorance, we are overwhelmed by them. The concept of AI as “the new aesthetic of fascism” is provocative but compelling. Nostalgia, idealized hierarchies, and fantasies of order are easily packaged into seductive visuals. Yet the reading also emphasizes that much AI slop is not ideologically coherent at all, it is engagement farming. This may be even more dangerous. When profit-driven algorithms prioritize whatever keeps users scrolling, they create an ecosystem where truth, fiction, propaganda, and absurdity are indistinguishable. The cheaper and more sensational the content, the more it thrives. Ultimately, the reflection left me with a sense of disorientation. The question “What can you believe if not your eyes?” lingers. In a media environment where images can no longer serve as stable evidence, our basic perceptual trust erodes. The tragedy is that this erosion does not produce radical skepticism or critical awakening. Instead, it produces numbness. We are not sleepwalking into disaster because we lack information, but because we are drowning in it, each crisis reduced to another spectacle in the infinite, algorithmic show.
